# 01 — Positioning & ICP

## One-line positioning

> **Elseware is the audio uniform of locking in. One purchase. Yours forever.**

That's the whole thing. Memorize it. Every piece of copy in every channel ladders up to that line.

## The three pillars positioning rests on

1. **Identity, not utility.** Endel sells you cognitive performance. Calm sells you peace. We sell you *the version of yourself that finally locks in.* When the user opens the app, they are *becoming* the person they want to be — disciplined, focused, in the zone — and the audio is the proof.
2. **Place, not playlist.** The unit of value is a *place*: Tokyo Kissaten, Lisbon Rainstorm, Library in Seoul, Trans-Siberian, Bali Morning, Tulum Beachfront. People don't buy "lo-fi with rain." They buy *being somewhere else*.
3. **Owned, not rented.** No subscription. One purchase. Yours forever. This single fact, repeated relentlessly, will close 30%+ of all paid conversions on its own.

## The ICP (3 named personas)

We don't write for "everyone who likes ambient sound." We write for these three.

### Persona 1: Maya, the locked-in pre-law junior

- 20, junior at a public university, living in a studio she just moved into.
- LSAT in 6 months. Working as a barista 3 shifts/week. GPA matters.
- Aesthetic: Pinterest "old money study," cream cardigans, Muji notebooks, a desk lamp with a warm bulb. Drinks oat-milk lattes. Hates the word "wellness."
- On Instagram 3+hrs/day. Saves Reels she'll never rewatch. Posts maybe 2 stories a week.
- Pain: she sits down to study and the silence is louder than the noise. Music with lyrics derails her. Her current playlist is a 10hr lo-fi YouTube video she resents.
- Trigger: she sees a Reel of someone's desk with a soft rain ambience and the caption "lockin mode for the next 90 minutes" — she wants the same audio, *now*.
- Why she pays: not because she "needs" it, but because installing the app and unlocking it is *the act of starting to study*. The purchase is part of the ritual.

### Persona 2: David, the 1am founder

- 31, building his second startup after the first failed.
- Married, no kids yet. Works from a shared bedroom-office. Wife sleeps at 11, he works until 2am.
- Listens to Brain.fm but resents the $99/year and the clinical UI.
- Aesthetic: a Herman Miller chair he can't really afford, an Eames desk lamp, mechanical keyboard, dark mode everything.
- Trigger: a friend shares a Reel that says "this app sounds like a Tokyo cafe at 11pm and it's $9 once" — he installs it before he finishes the Reel.
- Why he pays: subscription fatigue + sunk-cost-on-Brain.fm anger + the visual brand feels like *his taste*.

### Persona 3: Sarah, the writer/grad student hybrid

- 27, MFA in fiction, second-year. Day job teaching freshman comp.
- Writes between 6am and 8am every day, religiously, before classes.
- Reads Substack newsletters, listens to Tyler Cowen and Ezra Klein, follows three independent creators on IG.
- Aesthetic: Joan Didion-coded, linen, ceramics, "I shop at the farmer's market" energy. Wears noise-canceling headphones at the coffee shop.
- Trigger: a carousel post showing six "places" — Tokyo, Lisbon, Bali, Seoul, Tulum, Trans-Siberian — with cinematic stills. The caption is a sentence she'd quote-tweet. She's in the App Store before she finishes the carousel.
- Why she pays: identity-aligned aesthetics + the one-time price feels like buying a book, not subscribing to a streaming service.

## What all three share (the "ICP-essential" facts)

- **Ages 19–35**, students/early-career professionals/founders.
- **Heavy iPhone users**, secondary device often AirPods or noise-canceling headphones.
- **Already paying for at least 1 productivity-adjacent app** (Notion, Spotify, Calm, Roam) — they're not price-sensitive, they're trust-sensitive.
- **Saturated with subscription fatigue.**
- **Define themselves by what they're working on**, not by where they work.
- **Use Instagram as their primary source of aesthetic and identity inputs.** TikTok for entertainment, Instagram for the version of themselves they want to become.

## What we are NOT

We are not selling to:
- People who say "self-care."
- People who buy bath bombs.
- People searching "anxiety relief."
- People who don't already use focus apps.
- People who want a guided meditation.
- People who can be sold a subscription.

If a marketing tactic would convert any of those audiences, we don't run it. The discipline of who we *don't* talk to is half the brand.

## Voice & tone

The voice is **calm, disciplined, lightly dry, never cheerful, never preachy**. Think Ryan Holiday's tweets. Think the New Yorker capsule reviews. Think the way a barista at a third-wave coffee shop describes the morning's pour-over.

Voice rules:
- No emojis in captions. (We can use one ~ or → as a typographic accent.)
- No "✨ vibes ✨" energy. No "babe." No "girlies."
- No exclamation points. Ever. Period.
- Lowercase by default in IG captions. Title case for headlines.
- Sentence fragments are encouraged. Short. Like this.
- The word "lock in" appears in 70%+ of captions and Reel hooks. It's our cultural anchor.

## Words we use

- lock in / locked in / lock-in
- somewhere / elsewhere / Elseware
- atmosphere / ambient / soundscape (sparingly)
- preset / mix / channel
- ritual / cadence / discipline
- desk / studio / library / cafe / window
- own it / yours forever / one purchase

## Words we never use

- vibes (cringe)
- self-care (off-brand audience)
- relax (we're about working, not relaxing)
- mindfulness (clinical, distancing)
- meditation (wrong category)
- subscription (the enemy)
- premium (sounds like a tier; we don't have tiers)
- AI / generative (sounds like Endel; we're handcrafted)
- science / studies show (sounds like Brain.fm; we're aesthetic)

## The brand promise (single sentence we will deliver on every time)

> Open Elseware, choose a place, and within 30 seconds you are somewhere else and locked in.

If a feature, copy line, post, or DM doesn't reinforce that promise, we cut it. No exceptions.

## The competitive map (one paragraph)

Calm and Headspace own "wellness." Brain.fm and Endel own "neuroscience-backed focus." Lofi Girl owns "the YouTube background." Spotify owns "mood playlist." All of them are subscriptions. All of them are clinical, generic, or impersonal. The aesthetic, identity-driven, one-time-purchase niche between them — the one Maya, David, and Sarah live in — is empty. That's our wedge.

## The brand truth that earns the price

> An iPhone audio app for $9.99 *once* is so far below the value people derive from it that the price reads, subconsciously, as an aesthetic statement: "I'm not the kind of person who needs to be sold."

We never apologize for the price. We never discount. The price is part of the brand.
